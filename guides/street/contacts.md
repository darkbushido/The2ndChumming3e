# Contacts & Legwork
{: .no_toc }

Asking a contact what they know, paying for it, searching the Matrix
instead, and what each contact level adds. All rules are from the core
rulebook, *(SR3 p.61–62, p.253–256)*.

## Legwork: asking a contact  *(SR3 p.253)*
{: #legwork }

Legwork means following up clues by asking people. The book calls it "the
primary function of most Shadowrun contacts".

1. **Roll Etiquette.** Make a Success Test with **Etiquette (Street)** or
   **Etiquette (Corporate)**, whichever fits the contact, against
   **TN 4**. If the adventure has a success table for the question, use
   its target number. *(SR3 p.253–254)*
2. **Count successes.** The number of successes decides what the contact
   **knows and is willing to tell**, which can be two very different
   things. More successes **add** to what fewer would give: 3 successes
   get the 1-, 2- and 3-success entries. *(SR3 p.253)*
3. **Play the meeting.** The GM roleplays the encounter with that
   information level in mind. Contacts have their own lives and needs; a
   reporter may meet in a bar, a Mafia soldier in a back alley at 3 a.m.
   *(SR3 p.253)*
4. **Pay.** See [Paying for information](#paying).

Contacts stay trustworthy **as long as the runners play it safe**. Don't
reveal where the information came from, or make the contact choose
between you and the people they work with. *(SR3 p.253–254)*

### Writing a success table

Most published adventures include one. For your own mystery, write a line
for each success count, from vague to specific. For example, asking a
street shaman about a haunted block:

| Successes | The contact says |
| :---: | :--- |
| 0 | Nothing useful. |
| 1 | "The spirits around there are upset. Stay out after dark." |
| 2 | "It's a bound spirit, not a wild one. Somebody's using it." |
| 3+ | "A toxic shaman moved into the old water plant. That's who's binding it." |

The table above is an illustration, not a book table. In Foundry, the GM
can keep one as a Rollable Table and let the 🔎 Legwork tool read it (see
[Legwork in The 2nd Chumming](#legwork-tool)).

### Paying for information  *(SR3 p.254)*
{: #paying }

Whether a contact charges, and when they'll want a favor back, is the GM's
call. The book offers a formula:

> **Fee = contact's Charisma × contact's Intelligence × 50¥ ÷ successes
> on the Etiquette Test**

The GM adjusts it to suit the contact, and normal **Negotiation** sets the
final price (see [Buying Gear & Fencing](../gear-and-fencing/), step 4 of buying on
the street, for how that test works).

**Example.** A street shaman contact with Charisma 4 and Intelligence 5.
The runner rolls Etiquette (Street) against TN 4 and gets 2 successes:
4 × 5 × 50 = 1,000¥, ÷ 2 = **500¥** before negotiation. With 4 successes
the same answer, and more, would cost **250¥**.

### "Keep an ear to the ground"  *(SR3 p.254)*

A runner can ask a contact to check around and report back. The **GM**
makes an Etiquette Test **for the contact**, at **+2** to the success
table's target number. Any success means the contact reports that level of
information in **2D6 hours**, or whenever the GM decides. The fee uses the
same formula.

This is the book's way to make sure a clue the adventure needs reaches the
runners.

### Rushing a meeting  *(SR3 p.254)*

Contacts aren't always free when the runners want them. To rush a meeting,
the runner makes an **Etiquette Test** to get it at all, and the Etiquette
Test for the information then takes **+2 to its target number**.

## Asking the right contact  *(SR3 p.254)*
{: #appropriate-contacts }

A contact only knows what someone in their line of work would know. A
plainclothes cop hears precinct rumors but not a Mitsuhama secretary's
home address. A corporate contact may know patrol schedules and the
latest secret project, but not a gang's new turf.

The GM can make a contact "surprisingly multi-dimensional or
disappointingly narrow". A talismonger is a poor source for a gun; a chess
partner might know a Knight Errant officer's address. *(SR3 p.256)*

## Searching the Matrix instead  *(SR3 p.254)*

A decker can do legwork in the Matrix.

- **Test:** **Etiquette (Matrix)** against the success table's target
  number. **Hacking Pool can't be used.**
- **Time:** base time **2D6 hours**. Successes can be traded to cut the
  base time instead of buying information.
- **What it finds:** the decker says what kind of sources they search.
  Corporate databases give what a corporate contact would know; a street
  chat-line gives what a street contact would know.
- **How many at once:** up to **half Intelligence, rounded up**, searches
  at a time, if the decker does nothing else. Other activity cuts that
  down at the GM's discretion.

## Contact levels  *(SR3 p.254–256)*
{: #contact-levels }

| Level | Name | What they'll do | Bonus on Etiquette Tests for information | Covering for you when asked about the runner |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1** | Contact | An acquaintance. "May know information that a character needs and will probably reveal it", or may keep it to themself. | — | GM rolls **Intelligence (6)**, opposed, for whether they can **remember** you. They won't lie for you. |
| **2** | Buddy | Sees you semi-regularly. **Always** provides the information, and may keep an ear to the ground if they have time. Loyal unless it means sticking their neck out too far. | **+1 die** | GM rolls **Willpower (5)**, opposed, for whether they can **refuse** to answer. |
| **3** | Friend for life | Knows you well and sees you regularly. | **+2 dice** | GM rolls **Willpower (6)**, opposed, for whether they can **refuse** to answer. |

A Level 1 contact who "can't remember" the runner genuinely can't, and if
they do remember, they probably don't know where to find you.
*(SR3 p.254–256)*

### Getting contacts  *(SR3 p.61–62, p.254)*

- Every character starts with **two free Level 1 contacts**. *(SR3 p.61)*
- More can be bought **only at character creation**, on the Starting
  Character Extras Table. *(SR3 p.62)*

| Extra contact | Cost |
| :--- | ---: |
| Contact (Level 1) | 5,000¥ |
| Buddy (Level 2) | 10,000¥ |
| Friend For Life (Level 3) | 200,000¥ |

- After creation, contacts are gained **only through roleplaying**. New
  ones should be Level 1, rarely Level 2, and **never** Level 3.
  *(SR3 p.254)*

## Contacts with lives of their own  *(SR3 p.256)*

- **Flesh them out.** Give each contact an archetype title (Street Doc,
  Detective) and something going on in their life: a business in trouble,
  a daughter in with the wrong crowd. It colors how they answer, and can
  start a run.
- **They can turn the tables**: ask the runners for information, compete
  for the same data, be pressured into silence or into lying, or chat
  about weird happenings that turn out to be clues.
- **Favor for a friend.** A contact may ask the runners to do a job as a
  favor rather than for nuyen, which lets the players pick their own runs.

## Legwork in The 2nd Chumming
{: #legwork-tool }

The GM runs legwork with **🔎 Legwork**, one of the GM tools on the
**Rollable Tables** sidebar tab. Players don't have the button. Tell the GM
who you're asking and what about.

1. **Pick the runner and the contact.** The contact list is that runner's
   contacts. A Matrix search needs no contact.
2. **Pick what they do:** ask the contact, have the contact keep an ear to
   the ground, or search the Matrix.
3. **Check the numbers.** The dialog starts on Etiquette (Street), then
   Corporate, or Etiquette (Matrix) for a search. It adds the contact
   level's bonus dice when asking. The TN starts at 4, or at the chosen
   success table's. It adds +2 for a rushed meeting, +2 for an ear to the
   ground, and the runner's wounds. The GM can change any number.
4. **Roll.** On a TN of 7 or more, sixes are 💥 rerolls first. The result
   card shows the successes, **every row of the success table up to that
   many**, and the fee (Charisma × Intelligence × 50¥ ÷ successes) *(SR3 p.253–254)*. An ear
   to the ground also shows the 2D6 hours; a Matrix search shows its base
   time and how many searches the decker can run at once. The card goes to
   the GM only unless the GM unticks that.

The contact's Charisma and Intelligence come from its linked NPC or Little
Black Book archetype. A contact with no stat block leaves them blank for
the GM to fill in. The fee is only shown, never charged: Negotiation still
sets the price.

**Success tables.** The *GM: Legwork Success Tables* compendium (GM Only
folder) holds a template with rows for 1, 2 and 3+ successes. The GM
imports a copy for each mystery, writes the rows, and puts the TN in its
description as *Target Number: N*. Each row's range is the success count
that reveals it.

The rushed meeting's own Etiquette Test isn't rolled by the tool. Roll it
from the sheet first.

## Loyalty and Connection in The 2nd Chumming
{: #loyalty-and-connection }

{: .house }
> The Foundry system's contact item has **Loyalty** and **Connection**
> ratings (1–6). **They aren't in the SR3 core rulebook**, which describes
> contacts only by level. The character importer sets both to the
> contact's level, so Loyalty 1 / Connection 1 is a **Level 1 contact**.
> The 🔎 Legwork tool doesn't store a level either: the GM picks Level 1, 2
> or 3 in the dialog, and it starts at the contact's Loyalty (3 at most).
> How the two ratings should map to the book's levels is still open.
>
> If you want the ratings to mean something, one light reading: use
> **Connection** to cap how far down the success table the contact can
> reach, and **Loyalty** to decide whether they'll share anything that
> puts them at risk. That's a suggestion, not a rule.
