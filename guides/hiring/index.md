# Hiring & Service Costs
{: .no_toc }

What the books say about paying shadowrunners, and a house-rule framework
for pricing individual specialists. The eight specialist pages below build
on this one.

## What the books say

### Setting the fee  *(SRComp p.99)*

- Player characters average about **one shadowrun a month**.
- A good starting fee for **each runner** is **one month's living expenses
  plus the cost of the gear the run needs**. If the team's lifestyles differ,
  use the average.
- The GM decides in the end, based on the risk, what the employer can and
  will pay, and the runners' reputations.

### Baseline Shadowrun Payment Table  *(SRComp p.100)*

| Run | Bottom-line Fee |
| :--- | :--- |
| Assassination | 5,000¥ |
| Bodyguard/Security Duty | 200¥/day |
| Burglary | 2,000¥ |
| Courier Run | 1,000¥ |
| Datasteal | 20% value of data |
| Distraction | 1,000¥ |
| Destruction | 5,000¥ |
| Enforcement | 1,000¥ |
| Encryption/Decryption | 200¥ per MP |
| Extraction | 20,000¥ |
| Hacking | 1,000¥ x Host's Security Value |
| Investigation | 200¥/day |
| Smuggling Run | 5,000¥ |

How to use it *(SRComp p.99–100)*:

- These are **low-end** rates for **the task alone**. More work, more risk, a
  tougher target or higher-caliber runners should all push them up.
- **Bigger teams can generally expect more.** The rates don't account for
  team size.
- The rates **don't include expenses, hazard pay or "death benefits"**.
- In an amoral campaign, where the runners care more about money than doing
  the right thing, **double the base fees and windfalls**.
- A rare **windfall** should come to roughly **six months of the character's
  lifestyle**.
- Pay doesn't have to be cash. Valuable data, equipment, paid expenses, time
  in a magical research facility, Matrix access and SINs all work.

### How Mr. Johnson pays  *(MJLBB p.20–21)*

- Pay is fluid, and should suit the danger and the style of game. Johnson
  has a **budget** he won't go over, and he **lowballs** his first offer so
  there's room to haggle.
- The more information and support Johnson provides, the less he'll pay.
- Johnson normally covers **miscellaneous expenses** such as travel and visa
  fees, **if** they're negotiated. Runners who forget to ask may be out of
  luck.
- **Up-front money**: never more than **half**, and rarely more than a
  **third**.
- **Paying in gear**: a corporate Johnson can usually afford up to **25%
  over budget** if he pays in equipment. That gear may be tagged, trackable,
  or a prototype.

### What living costs  *(SR3 p.240)*

| Lifestyle | Per month |
| :--- | ---: |
| Street | Free |
| Squatter | 100¥ |
| Low | 1,000¥ |
| Middle | 5,000¥ |
| High | 10,000¥ |
| Luxury | 100,000¥ and up |
| Hospitalized | 500¥ per day (1,000¥ in intensive care) |

So by the Companion's rule, a Low-lifestyle runner's fee starts near
**1,000¥ plus gear** for a run, and a High-lifestyle runner's near
**10,000¥ plus gear**.

## The house-rule framework

{: .house }
> The specialist pages use this structure. It came from the original gists.
> The numbers are **not from any SR3 book**, and are **much higher** than the
> Companion's baselines. For example, a "professional" bodyguard at
> 1,500¥ a night compares with the book's **200¥ a day**. They fit a
> high-paying campaign. Scale them to your table.

**Fee = base day rate × threat multiplier, plus hazard pay (a percentage of
that), plus expenses.**

1. **Base day rate** is set by the specialist's tier in their main skill
   (1–3, 4–5, 6–7, 8+).
2. **Threat multiplier**, from about ×1 for routine work to ×3–×6 for heavy
   opposition.
3. **Hazard pay** for being on-site rather than working remotely, usually
   +50% to +100%.
4. **Expenses and deposits**: ammunition, reagents, fake IDs, drone and deck
   repairs. By the book, Johnson covers expenses only if they're negotiated
   *(MJLBB p.20–21)*.

Fixers take a finder's fee, which the books don't quantify. The original
gists suggested **10–30%** depending on how rare the specialist is.

Many top-tier freelancers will ask instead for **an equal share** of the
team's payout. That fits the Companion's guidance that the fee is per
runner *(SRComp p.99)*.
