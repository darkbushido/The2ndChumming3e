# The Sixth World: who runs it
{: .no_toc }

The megacorporations, the syndicates, the policlubs, the governments and
the dragons, and how they deal with each other. That web of rivalries is
where shadowruns come from.

## How the corps got above the law  *(SR3 p.22)*

Two US Supreme Court rulings at the turn of the century made the modern
megacorp:

- **The Seretech decision** upheld a corporation's right to keep an
  **armed force** to protect its people and property. That legitimised
  private corporate armies.
- **The Shiawase decision** established **corporate extraterritoriality**:
  a multinational gets the same rights and privileges as a foreign
  government. Its land is effectively foreign soil.

The rest follows from those two rulings. A corp facility is a sovereign
enclave with its own army, so the police can't simply walk in, and no
government can easily make a corp answer for anything. Nations still find
ways to push back. The Confederated American States keeps a registry of
extraterritorial property and an "eviction clause" it has used only once
*(SOTA64 p.21)*.

The SR3 introduction sums up the result. Megacorps are "a law unto
themselves", the nuyen is the world's currency, and when a corp wants a job
done without dirtying its hands, it hires shadowrunners *(SR3 p.8)*.

## The Corporate Court: the referee
{: #the-corporate-court-the-referee }

The biggest corps answer, loosely, to one body: the **Corporate Court**,
which they sit on themselves.

- **It owns the bank.** In 2032–33 the Court took over Global Financial
  Services, which had replaced the World Bank. It moved GFS into orbit as
  the **Zurich-Orbital Gemeinschaft Bank** *(SR3 p.31)*.
- **It acts for the corps together.** In 2048 Aztlan nationalised every
  foreign business. The corps answered with **Operation Reciprocity**, a
  combined corporate military strike on Aztechnology troops in Ensenada.
  The **Veracruz Settlement** that followed compensated the other corps and
  let them back into Aztlan, provided Aztlan citizens held the major
  shares of their local subsidiaries. The same year, the **Panama Canal** became a
  Pan-Corporate Zone under the Court's authority *(SR3 p.31)*.
- **A seat on it is fiercely contested.** Yamatetsu spent 2041 fighting for
  a seat on the Court and on the Zurich-Orbital board. When it won, the
  "Big Seven" became the **Big Eight** *(SR3 p.31)*. When the Fuchi and
  Renraku representatives died in 2059–60, both seats went to newcomers,
  **Wuxing** and **Cross Applied Technologies**, rather than to Big Eight
  corps *(SR3 p.35)*.
- **It decides who owns what.** After Fuchi broke up, Novatech, Renraku and
  Shiawase all claimed its subsidiaries, and the Court had not yet ruled.
  In the meantime, each corp worked to make its own claim the strongest
  *(NS p.95)*.

**What that means for runners:** the Court keeps corp-on-corp fighting
**deniable**. An open war risks the others ganging up on you, as they did on
Aztlan. A shadowrun that nobody can trace doesn't.

## How the factions deal with each other

### Corp against corp: the shadow war

- **Deniable assets.** A corporate Mr. Johnson is paid from black-ops
  accounts, keeps his employer secret, and knows he'll be disowned if he's
  exposed *(MJLBB p.17–18)*. Corporate Johnsons are the most common kind
  *(MJLBB p.17)*.
- **Raids on rivals' people and research.** The defining example of the
  period is the war between **Fuchi and Renraku**. In 2058, Dunkelzahn's
  will gave Fuchi's security chief, Miles Lanier, a seat on Renraku's board.
  Fuchi split into three warring factions, and by 2060 it had broken apart.
  Villiers took his third to **Novatech**, and the other two were absorbed
  by Renraku and Shiawase *(SR3 p.34–35)*.
- **Keeping newcomers out.** The Big Eight worked to crush smaller corps
  that Dunkelzahn's will had made rich, "with much resulting business for
  shadowrunners". Lucien Cross of Cross Applied Technologies survived
  three assassination attempts in 2058 alone *(SR3 p.35)*.
- **Infighting within a corp.** Most corps fight themselves as well.
  Shiawase's divisions compete for head-office money, and some of that
  rivalry ends in shadowruns *(NS p.95)*. The Telestrian family is split
  into factions *(NS p.88)*. Yamatetsu's traditionalists resist its ork
  chairman *(NS p.96)*.

### Corps in one city: the United Corporate Council  *(NS p.83)*

Seattle's corps present one face to the city government through the
**United Corporate Council**. It's an advisory body to the governor that
pushes the corporate agenda. It was formed in 2030, after a corp war in
which Aztechnology wiped out Campana & Carrindum, so that such
"misunderstandings" wouldn't happen again. *New Seattle* calls it a
small-scale version of the Corporate Court. Its members include the
megacorps, the big local corps, Lone Star and the Draco Foundation. The
backstabbing inside it is constant but subtle. A member that moves too
openly risks the rest ganging up on it, so they hire runners for
**paydata**, to give their lobbyists an edge.

### Corps and syndicates

- **Hired muscle.** Mitsuhama used to call on the Seattle yakuza for
  "special problems". After the yakuza there broke with Japan, Mitsuhama
  had to hire more shadowrunners instead *(NS p.92–93)*.
- **The yakuza inside the boardroom.** The yakuza's *sokaiya* branch
  combines stock purchases with blackmail to steer corporations and skim
  their money *(NS p.102)*.
- **The Triads and the Pacific Prosperity Group.** Wuxing is the driving
  force behind the PPG, a trade bloc against Japanese economic dominance.
  Runners claim the Triads will target Wuxing's rivals, Mitsuhama above
  all *(NS p.96)*.

### Corps and governments

- **Contracts are power.** Seattle's power grid, telecommunications,
  public databases and traffic grid are all run by corporations under
  contract *(NS p.76)*. So are its police *(NS p.76)*.
- **States own corps too.** **Gaeatronics** is owned by the Salish-Shidhe
  Council *(NS p.84)*. **Telestrian Industries** is "the Tir government's
  proxy in Seattle", runners claim, because the Council of Princes owns 5%
  of it *(NS p.87–88)*.
- **Governments ban corps.** Aztechnology is barred from doing business in
  California and Tir Tairngire *(NS p.81, p.90)*, and so is
  **Saeder-Krupp** *(NS p.95)*. Both run their interests there through
  shells and watchers based in Seattle.
- **And corps run cities.** New York was rebuilt by the corps in exchange
  for concessions that leave them running it. Los Angeles's leaders "dance
  to corporate hymns" *(SR3 p.30)*.

### Policlubs and everyone

Policlubs keep their hands clean by funding terrorists. **Humanis** quietly
pays groups like Alamos 20,000 to do the violence it publicly denies
*(NS p.79)*. Runners claim **ORC** leaders pass resources to pro-Awakened
terror groups *(NS p.80)*. Both have rich backers. Kenneth Brackhaven, a
corporate investor and Humanis leader Karl Brackhaven's nephew, has paid
for the campaigns of at least six district mayors *(NS p.83–84)*. See
[Politics, Police & Policlubs](politics-and-security/#policlubs).

### The dragons

- **Lofwyr** announced in 2037 that he owned 63% of the company that became
  **Saeder-Krupp**, and made himself its chief executive *(SR3 p.31)*.
- **Dunkelzahn** was elected UCAS president and assassinated on his
  inauguration night. His **will** founded the **Draco Foundation**, and its
  individual bequests started trouble everywhere: it was "a master stroke
  of manipulation from beyond the grave" *(SR3 p.34)*.
- Runners speculate that the great dragons **Lung** and **Ryumyo** are
  playing a chess game along the Pacific Rim *(NS p.104)*. It's rumor, but
  it's useful for a GM.

## Using this at the table

{: .note }
> These are suggestions, not rules.

- **Ask who gains.** Most runs in these books trace back to one faction
  hurting another. Before a run, decide who the Johnson really works for,
  which rival is the real target, and who else is watching. The Johnson
  types are covered in *(MJLBB p.17–18)*.
- **Use the rivalries listed above.** Fuchi's leftovers (Novatech, Renraku
  and Shiawase), the Seattle yakuza's split from Japan, the Mafia's
  succession fight, and Humanis against the Empowerment Coalition are all
  open conflicts in 2060–61, and each one hires runners.
- **Expect retaliation.** Mitsuhama, runners say, "makes examples of runners
  who double-cross them" *(NS p.93)*. Cross Corp tags intruding deckers and
  comes to collect favours weeks later *(NS p.91)*.
