# Karma
{: .no_toc }

How Karma is earned, how it splits into **Good Karma** and the **Karma Pool**, what each one buys,
and how the Karma Pool refreshes. From the core rulebook, *(SR3 p.242–248)*, with the magical
purchases from the Magic chapter *(SR3 p.168, p.180–181, p.190)*.

1. TOC
{:toc}

---

## Two kinds of Karma  *(SR3 p.242)*

Karma is awarded at the end of an adventure, not necessarily after each session. Once awarded it
splits in two:

- **Good Karma** is spent over time to improve the character: Attributes, skills, spells, foci.
- **Karma Pool** is spent during play to get out of trouble: re-rolls, extra dice, avoiding a
  disaster.

Every point awarded goes to Good Karma **except every twentieth point, which goes to the Karma Pool
instead**. **Humans add every tenth point** to their Karma Pool. Every character starts with a Karma
Pool of **1** *(SR3 p.244)*. Track Good Karma, Karma Pool and **Total Karma** (everything ever
awarded) separately *(SR3 p.242)*.

**Worked example, a human** *(SR3 p.242, p.244)*: Brynn starts with Karma Pool 1. She is awarded
12 Karma. The tenth point goes to her Pool, which is now **2**, and the other 11 are Good Karma. She
later earns 9 more, for a Total of 21. The twentieth point goes to her Pool, which is now **3**, and
the other 8 to Good Karma: 11 + 8 = **19**.

**Worked example, an elf** *(SR3 p.244)*: Shetani has a Total Karma of **62**. Every twentieth
point went to the Pool: 20, 40 and 60 are three points, plus the 1 he started with, so his Pool is
**4**. The other 59 went to Good Karma, of which he has spent 49, leaving **10**.

## Earning Karma  *(SR3 p.242, p.244)*

Each surviving member of the team gets Karma for staying alive, for succeeding at the mission and
for how dangerous it was. On top of that the gamemaster gives individual awards:

| For | Award *(SR3 p.244)* |
| :--- | :--- |
| Good roleplaying (mostly stayed in character) | 1; excellent roleplaying 2 |
| Guts — brave, effective fighting (not stupidly brave) | 1; particularly heroic 2 |
| Smarts — a clever plan, a solved clue, a good scam, knowing when to run | at least 1 |
| Motivation — driving the story forward | 1 |
| Right place, right time — the vital skill, not known in advance to be needed | 1 |
| Surprise — a strategy that foils the gamemaster's plans | 1 |
| Humour and drama, in character | 1 |

An amazing run might earn one character 10 or 12 Karma. More than 12 for a single adventure is
"highly unlikely" *(SR3 p.244)*. Good dice rolls alone earn nothing.

## Spending Good Karma

Good Karma is spent **between adventures** *(SR3 p.244)*. The gamemaster can also require training
time, a teacher, or a test to learn or improve something *(SR3 p.245)*.

### Attributes  *(SR3 p.244)*

Raising a Physical or Mental Attribute by 1 costs **2 × the new rating**, using the natural,
unaugmented rating. Above the **Racial Modified Limit**, up to the **Attribute Maximum** (the limit
× 1.5), it costs **3 × the new rating**. Strength 5 → 6 costs **12**; an elf's Strength 6 → 7, over
her limit of 6, costs **21**.

Good Karma can't raise **Essence** or **Magic**, even if they were reduced. **Reaction** isn't raised
directly: raise Quickness and Intelligence instead.

### Skills  *(SR3 p.244–245)*

A **new skill** costs **1** at rating 1, whatever its kind. After that, multiply the number on the
table by the **new** rating and **round down**. The multiplier depends on the skill's **linked
Attribute** (natural rating) at the time you buy.

**Skill Improvement Cost Table** *(SR3 p.245)*

| New rating is… | Active Skill | Knowledge / Language Skill |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Less than or equal to the Attribute | 1.5 | 1 |
| Less than or equal to 2 × the Attribute | 2 | 1.5 |
| More than 2 × the Attribute | 2.5 | 2 |

| New specialization rating is… | Active | Knowledge / Language |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Less than or equal to the Attribute | .5 | .5 |
| Less than or equal to 2 × the Attribute | 1 | 1 |
| More than 2 × the Attribute | 1.5 | 1.5 |

**Worked example** *(SR3 p.245)*: Iris has Pistols 3 and Quickness 4. Pistols 4 costs 4 × 1.5 =
**6**. Pistols 5 is over her Quickness, so it costs 5 × 2 = **10**.

### Specializations  *(SR3 p.84, p.245)*

- A new specialization is bought **one point above the base skill**, as if it were already at the
  base skill's rating and being raised. The base skill isn't reduced.
- You can have as many specializations of a skill as its **linked Attribute's rating**.
- A specialization can't be more than **twice its base skill** (a base skill of 1 may have a
  specialization of 3). Raise the base skill first.
- Specializations don't rise when the base skill does; each is raised separately.

**Worked example** *(SR3 p.245)*: Brick has Stealth 5 and Quickness 6. A new Sneaking specialization
starts at 6: 6 × .5 = **3**. Raising it to 7, over his Quickness, would cost 7 × 1 = **7**.

There are no refunds if a later Attribute increase would have made a purchase cheaper *(SR3 p.245)*.

### Magic  *(SR3 p.168, p.180–181, p.190)*

| Purchase | Good Karma |
| :--- | :--- |
| An adept's extra **Power Point**, during play *(SR3 p.168)* | **20** each |
| **Learning a spell** *(SR3 p.180)* | the desired **Force** (a failed attempt costs no Karma) |
| …with a **limit** (fetish, exclusive) taken on the cost *(SR3 p.180)* | the Force **minus** the limit's modifier |
| **Bonding a focus** *(SR3 p.190)* | from the table below |

**Focus Bonding Table** *(SR3 p.190)*

| Item | Karma cost |
| :--- | :--- |
| Expendable Spell Focus | 0 |
| Specific Spell Focus | 1 × Force |
| Spell Category Focus | 3 × Force |
| Spirit Focus | 2 × Force |
| Power Focus | 5 × Force |
| Sustaining Focus | 1 × Force |
| Weapon Focus | (3 + Weapon Reach) × Force |

**Worked example** *(SR3 p.181)*: learning Fireball at Force 8 with a −2 limit taken on the cost
costs 8 − 2 = **6** Karma.

Initiation, quickening, allies and the rest of *Magic in the Shadows* also cost Good Karma; they
aren't covered here yet.

## Using the Karma Pool  *(SR3 p.246)*

The Karma Pool is spent **during play**. Some uses **spend** points, which come back when the pool
refreshes. Others **burn** them, which removes them permanently: *"burned Karma Pool dice do not
refresh"* *(SR3 p.246)*.

| Use *(SR3 p.246)* | Cost | Spent or burned |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Re-roll failures** — re-roll every die in a test that failed | 1 the first time; 2 more for a second re-roll of the same test, 3 more for a third… | spent |
| **Avoid a disaster** — every die came up 1 *(Rule of One, SR3 p.38)* | 1: the disaster becomes a simple failure | spent |
| **Open Test re-roll** | 1 for one die, 2 more for a second (3 in all), 3 more for a third (6)… | spent |
| **Buy additional dice** — up to the skill, Attribute or rating dice you're using, not counting dice from other pools | 1 for one die, 2 more for a second, 3 more for a third… | spent |
| **Buy successes** — only if the test already scored at least one | 1 per success | **burned** |
| **Extended actions** — Karma Pool committed when a long task begins *(SR3 p.246, 248; gamemaster's option)* | as committed; the pool doesn't refresh until the task is done | spent |
| **The Hooper-Nelson rule** — lower a spectacularly difficult target number *(SR3 p.248; gamemaster's option)* | 1 per point of TN; recommended only at TN 12 or more, and never below 8 | **burned** |

A few limits worth knowing:

- **A success can't be re-rolled.** Only the failed dice go again.
- **An "oops" roll can't be re-rolled.** On a Rule of One, the only use is avoiding the disaster.
- **Each re-roll of the same test costs more.** Re-rolling twice costs 1 + 2 = 3 in all; three
  times, 6.

**Worked example** *(SR3 p.246)*: Dodger rolls Electronics B/R 5 against TN 8 and gets one success.
He needs four, so he spends 1 Karma Pool to re-roll the four failed dice.

### Refreshing the pool  *(SR3 p.104, p.246)*

The Karma Pool **doesn't refresh every Combat Turn** like other dice pools. It refreshes when the
gamemaster says so, roughly once per **scene** of the story: every 24 hours of game time, or a
matter of hours or days *(SR3 p.104, p.246)*. In most games it refreshes at the **start of each
session**. The gamemaster can bend this: characters on the run may not get to refresh, a breather
between encounters may refresh some or all of it, and one character can refresh faster than
another. A character near death might not refresh until healed, while one pulling off heroic
stunts might refresh in short order *(SR3 p.246)*. Only what was **spent** comes back. Burned
points are gone.

## Doing it in The 2nd Chumming (Foundry)

The system tracks everything above and charges it when you use it, but nothing is refused: the
gamemaster can edit any number.

**On the sheet**
- **Bio → Resources** holds Good Karma and Total Karma, with **Spend Karma…** for players and
  **Award Karma…** for the gamemaster. Awards split themselves: the tenth or twentieth point goes to
  the Pool.
- **Attributes → Derived Pools** shows the Karma Pool as **left / size**. Spending lowers "left";
  burning lowers the size.
- The **📒 Ledger** on the Bio tab records every change, with the reason: "Pool spent" for spends,
  "Karma Pool" for burns.

**Spend Karma…** lists every purchase you can afford, priced from the tables above: Attributes (it
says when you're over the Racial Modified Limit), skills, new skills, specializations (an amber
warning past twice the base skill), and under **Magic** an adept's Power Point, learning a spell
(you choose the Force and any limit) and bonding a focus. The gamemaster can **↺ undo** any
purchase from its ledger line: the Karma comes back and the purchase is reversed, recorded as a new
line.

**On a roll**, every roll dialog has a 🍀 row:
- **Buy extra dice**: the cost (1, 3, 6…) is spent as the roll is made.
- **Hold the roll for Karma**: the finished dice wait on a 🍀 card before anything happens. From
  there you can re-roll the failures, avoid a disaster, or buy successes, then press **✔ Done**. Only
  you (and the gamemaster) can press those buttons.
- **Hooper-Nelson**: burn points to lower the TN, never below 8. It warns under TN 12, because it's
  the gamemaster's option.

**Refreshing** is the gamemaster's: **🍀 Refresh** on a character's sheet, or **🍀 Refresh Karma
Pools** among the GM tools (Rollable Tables tab) for the whole party. **Reset All Pools** doesn't touch
the Karma Pool.

Not yet in the system: Karma Pool on opposed tests (melee, astral, contested), on Damage Resistance
and Drain rolls, Open Test re-rolls, and *Magic in the Shadows'* Good Karma uses.
