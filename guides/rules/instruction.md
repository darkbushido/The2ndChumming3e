# Instruction & Learning Skills
{: .no_toc }

How a character learns a new skill or improves one: the **Good Karma** it costs, the **training** the gamemaster
may require, a **teacher** with the Instruction skill, and the **virtual instructor** programs and chips sold on the
Matrix. Karma costs are on the [Karma](../karma/#skills) page and aren't repeated here. All rules are from the
core rulebook, *(SR3 p.87, p.95, p.245, p.296)*.

1. TOC
{:toc}

---

## Learning a new skill  *(SR3 p.245)*

A new skill is bought at **rating 1** for **1 Good Karma**, whether it is an Active, Knowledge or Language skill.
Raising it past 1 follows the improvement costs on [Karma](../karma/#skills).

**The gamemaster controls how fast.** *"Gamemasters should not allow characters to simply spend large amounts of Karma
and become vastly more powerful almost overnight."* Characters should train, or use and improve their skills over time,
and may need to **search out a teacher** *(SR3 p.245)*. The book leaves the balance to the gamemaster and offers two ways:

| Option | How it works |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A short, simple mechanic** | Make a **Skill Test** using the skill, against a target number of **twice the Skill Rating sought**. A character without the skill **defaults**. An instructor **adds dice** to this test (see below). Good Karma can be spent to learn or raise the skill **only after this test succeeds**. |
| **Roleplay it** | Make the training the basis of an adventure: the instructor was just the target of a corporate extraction, a shaman's totem demands a quest before the character can improve at conjuring. |

*Worked example (arithmetic from the rule, not from the book):* a character wants **Pistols 4**. The test target number
is 2 × 4 = **8**. A teacher with the Instruction skill adds dice (below). Only after a success does the character spend
the **6 Good Karma** the [Karma](../karma/#skills) page works out for Pistols 4 at Quickness 4.

## The Instruction skill  *(SR3 p.87, p.95)*

**Instruction** is a Charisma-linked Active skill. It **defaults to the Charisma Attribute**, and its specializations
are **by specific subject** *(SR3 p.87)*.

- To teach a skill, spell or other subject, the instructor must have that skill at **Rating 3 or higher**. They must
  also have it **at or above the rating the pupil wants to reach** *(SR3 p.95)*.
- Teaching requires an **Instruction (4) Test**. For **every 2 successes**, the student gets **one additional die** on the
  test to learn the skill *(SR3 p.95)*.
- The instructor's effect is on the **pupil's learning test**, so it pairs with the Skill Test option above.
- Teaching spells works the same way. See [Spells](../../magic/spells/) for the teacher's fee and study time.

## Virtual instructors  *(SR3 p.95, p.296)*

*"Numerous 'virtual instructor' programs are now available widely, especially through the Matrix."* Each is a program with
**a rating in the skill it teaches, and a rating in Instruction**.

1. **Size.** Add the two ratings together and read the **Program Size Table** *(SR3 p.223)* with a **multiplier of 3**.
   The size is the total squared, times 3.
2. **Price.** For a program, use the **Program Costs and Availability Table** *(SR3 p.304)*: the rating band sets the
   Availability, Street Index and cost per Mp of size.
3. **As a chip.** A **Virtual Instructor Chip** is sold for **Mp × 100¥**, Availability **(ratings total)/72 hrs**,
   Street Index **1** *(SR3 p.296)*.

**Worked example** (the sum and the squaring are from the book's rule; the rest is the arithmetic):
Pistols 4 and Instruction 3 give a total of **7**. The Program Size Table's rating 7 row is 49, and **49 × 3 = 147 Mp**.
As a **chip**: 147 Mp × 100¥ = **14,700¥**, Availability **7/72 hrs**, Street Index 1.

### Restricted skills  *(SR3 p.95)*

*"Programs for illegal or restricted skills (such as Demolitions) typically have **twice the Street Index** and an
**Availability of 10 or higher**."*

### Legality  *(SR3 p.296)*

The Virtual Instructor Chip's Legality is printed as **`Legal/3-Y`** on the Skillsofts and Chips table. The category **Y**
is on the fines table: see [Legality Codes & Permits](../../street/legality/).

{: .house }
> **How the system reads the split.** The book prints both values and doesn't spell out when each applies. The system reads it
> as: **Legal** for an ordinary skill, **3-Y** for the illegal or restricted skills that page 95 singles out. It's recorded
> on the chips as "Legal (3-Y for illegal or restricted skills)", and the GM decides which skills count.

## Skillsofts and chips  *(SR3 p.295–296)*

Virtual instructors are one of the products on the **Skillsofts and Chips** table. The others (Activesoft, Datasoft,
Knowsoft, Linguasoft, Skillsoft Jukebox, Optical Memory Chip, Autonav Map Chip and the BTL chips) are
sold as chips with their own price, Availability and Legality.

- **Softs don't provide dice pools.** A Pistols activesoft doesn't give Combat Pool dice for Pistols Tests unless the
  character has the skill *(SR3 p.296)*.
- **Magic skills** such as Sorcery and Conjuring can't be made into activesofts *(SR3 p.296)*.
- **Memory** a skillsoft takes up comes from the Program Size Table *(SR3 p.223)*: a **multiplier of 3** for a base skill
  and **2** for a specialization *(SR3 p.296)*.
