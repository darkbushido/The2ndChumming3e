  • SR3 security codes are Blue, Green, Orange and Red, plus unofficial “UV”. There are no “Yellow” systems, and “Passive / Passive-Alert IC” is not an IC type. (SR3 p.205)
  • Blaster, sparky and tar pit are gray IC: they damage the deck and its programs. Black IC is the kind that hurts the decker, including the psychotropic variety. (SR3 p.227–230)
  • The “Scenario C” total was right. The others are restated below with the formula from the hiring overview.

What the decker is risking

  • Gray IC (blaster, ripper, sparky, tar pit) damages the deck and its programs. Burned chips have to be replaced. (SR3 p.228–229)
  • Lethal black IC damages the decker, at (IC Rating) Moderate on Blue and Green hosts and Serious on Orange and Red hosts. Once it hits, jacking out takes a Complex Action and a Willpower test. (SR3 p.230)
  • Decks are expensive. (SR3 p.304)

House rates

These rates aren’t from any SR3 book. They are the original gist’s numbers, kept for convenience.

Base day rate, by Computer skill

Tier Computer Base day rate Typical kit
Amateur 1–3 250–500¥ Entry deck, low MPCP
Professional 4–5 1,000–2,500¥ Mid-range deck; comfortable on Blue and Green hosts
Veteran 6–7 3,000–6,000¥ High-end or custom deck with strong utilities
Elite 8+ 10,000¥ and up Custom hardware and custom-coded utilities

Threat multiplier, by host security

Host Multiplier Why
Blue / Green ×1 Mostly white IC; being traced or dumped is the main risk
Orange ×1.5–2 Gray IC; deck damage becomes likely
Red ×3–5 Black IC; the decker can die
UV / megacorp core ×10, or a flat 50,000¥ and up Off the chart

Hazard pay and expenses

  • Remote decking from a safehouse: no hazard pay.
  • On-site decking, jacking in with the team: +100% to +200%.
  • Custom utilities written for the job should be paid for by the employer.
  • A deck repair deposit of 5,000–20,000¥ is reasonable against gray IC.
