Primer for the Awakened

What a starting magician can do, how astral space works, and what an elven mage in Seattle knows about the world.

Original gist: “Primer for the Awakened Elf”.

  • Drain codes like “4L, 3M, 5H” don’t exist. SR3 writes Drain as +1(M), meaning Power = Force ÷ 2 + 1 at Moderate. There is no “H” level.
  • Force is capped by the Force you learned the spell at, not by your Magic. Casting above your Magic is legal; the Drain just becomes Physical.
  • Nature spirits come in four families: Man, Land, Sky and Waters. “Aura Masking” is not a spirit service.
  • SR3 ritual sorcery uses a material link. “Sympathetic link” is a term from a later edition.
  • Astral attacks use an Astral Combat Pool and Sorcery, Unarmed Combat or an armed skill with a weapon focus. The gist’s “Astral Combat + Combat Pool” was wrong. Force / Magic is not astral armor.
  • Projecting movement: normal speed is Intelligence × 4 m per turn, and fast movement is up to Magic in kilometers per turn, not a flat 100 km per turn.
  • The Awakening is dated 24 December 2011, when the dragon Ryumyo appeared near Mount Fuji. It was not 21 December.

1. What a magician can do

Spellcasting

  • Spells fall into five categories: Combat, Detection, Health, Illusion, Manipulation. (SR3 p.191–198)
  • You learn each spell at a specific Force. You can cast it at that Force or lower, never higher. (SR3 p.178) At character creation no spell can be bought above Force 6. (SR3 p.60)
  • Casting is a Complex Action: roll Sorcery plus up to as many Spell Pool dice as Sorcery dice. The TN is the target’s Willpower (mana spells) or Body (physical spells), or its Object Resistance. (SR3 p.107, p.182)
  • Drain: Power = Force ÷ 2 (round down) + the spell’s modifier, at the Damage Level shown. Resist with Willpower plus any number of Spell Pool dice, and reduce one level per 2 successes. (SR3 p.162, p.44)
    • It’s Stun if the Force is no higher than your Magic, and Physical if it is higher, or if you’re astrally projecting. (SR3 p.162)
    • Every spell you’re sustaining adds +2 to Drain Power. (SR3 p.162)
  • Spell Pool = (INT + WIL + MAG) ÷ 3. (SR3 p.44)

For the full step-by-step, including resistance, Spell Defense and Drain, see Spellcasting, Resistance & Drain.

Conjuring

Hermetic mages call elementals: Earth, Air, Fire and Water. (SR3 p.186)

  • You need a conjuring library and a hermetic circle, each rated at least the Force of the elemental, plus ritual materials costing about 1,000¥ × Force. (SR3 p.186; MitS p.169)
  • The elemental needs something to form from: a bonfire, a pool of water, and so on. (SR3 p.186)
  • An elemental can give five services: Aid Sorcery, Aid Study, Spell Sustaining, Physical Service, Remote Service. For Aid Sorcery, fire elementals help combat spells, air detection, water illusion and earth manipulation. No elemental helps health spells. (SR3 p.187)

Shamans summon nature spirits, and only inside the spirit’s domain. (SR3 p.184, p.186)

  • Summoning is an Exclusive Complex Action. The Conjuring Test is against TN = the spirit’s Force, and each success is one service. With no successes, nothing comes, but Drain still applies. (SR3 p.186)
  • Nature spirits vanish at sunrise and sunset, and any unused services are lost. (SR3 p.186)
  • A service is one use of one of the spirit’s powers, or a physical task such as attacking. (SR3 p.186)
  • The four families are (SR3 p.266–267):
    • Spirits of Man: city, field, hearth
    • Spirits of the Land: desert, forest, mountain, prairie
    • Spirits of the Sky: mist, storm, wind
    • Spirits of the Waters: lake, river, sea, swamp

Conjuring Drain: Power = the spirit’s Force, with the level set by comparing Force to your Charisma. Resist with Charisma. (SR3 p.162, p.187)

Ritual sorcery (MitS p.34–36)

  • A team of magicians can cast one spell together. It builds over hours, but it can reach a target out of sight.
  • The team can have at most as many members as the lowest Sorcery rating among them. Everyone must know the spell at the chosen Force, and all must share a tradition (shamans of different totems may mix).
  • You need a hermetic circle or shamanic lodge rated at least the spell’s Force, plus ritual materials, which are used up whatever happens.
  • To reach a distant target you need a material link: a piece of the target, such as blood, hair or tissue.
  • Elemental manipulation spells can’t be cast as ritual sorcery.

Foci (SR3 p.189–191; MitS p.169)

Foci are bonded with Karma. The core types are spell foci (one specific spell or a whole category), spirit foci, power foci, sustaining foci and weapon foci. Active foci are visible and vulnerable on the astral. A weapon focus lets you use an armed skill in astral combat.

2. Astral space

Astral perception (SR3 p.171–172)

  • It takes a Simple Action to switch. While perceiving you’re dual-natured: you see astral forms and auras, and they can attack you.
  • Assensing: Intelligence against TN 4, taking a Simple Action. With 1–2 successes you learn general health, whether cyberware is present, rough emotions, and mundane or Awakened. More successes give Essence and Magic compared to yours, implant locations and astral signatures. (SR3 p.172)
  • Doing ordinary, non-magical tasks while perceiving (shooting, driving) costs +2 TN. (SR3 p.172)

Astral projection (SR3 p.172–173)

  • Leaving your body and returning are each an Exclusive Complex Action. Your body lies in a trance.
  • Your body loses 1 Essence per hour you’re away. At 0 Essence you die. (SR3 p.173)
  • Normal movement is Intelligence × 4 m per turn. Fast movement is up to Magic in kilometers per turn, roughly Magic × 1,000 km per hour. (SR3 p.173)
  • Solid earth blocks astral forms, which is why secure sites are often built underground. (SR3 p.173)

Astral attributes (SR3 p.173–174)

Physical While projecting
Strength Charisma
Quickness Intelligence
Body Willpower
Reaction Intelligence, and Initiative is INT + 20 + 1D6

Astral combat (SR3 p.174–175)

  • Astral combat uses the melee rules. There are no ranged attacks except spells.
  • A projecting character uses the Astral Combat Pool, (INT + WIL + CHA) ÷ 2. (SR3 p.44, p.174) Attack with an armed skill if you have a weapon focus, Unarmed Combat otherwise, or Sorcery in its place.
  • Damage: unarmed (Charisma)M; armed, Charisma plus the weapon focus’s damage; spirits, foci and barriers (Force)M. (SR3 p.175)
  • Worn armor is useless on the astral. Natural armor, such as a troll’s or an adept’s Mystic Armor, still counts. (SR3 p.172, p.175)
  • The attacker picks Stun or Physical damage. Wounds appear on your physical body. If your astral form dies, your body dies. (SR3 p.175)
  • Wards and other astral barriers block astral movement, and the only way through is to beat the barrier in astral combat. (SR3 p.174)

3. What a starting elven mage knows

The Awakened world

  • The Awakening happened in 2011. On 24 December, passengers on a bullet train saw the great dragon Ryumyo appear near Mount Fuji. (SR3 p.25)
  • Traditions: mages treat magic as a discipline of formulae and elements. (SR3 p.158) Shamans follow a totem, which grants bonus dice for some spells and spirits and penalties for others. (SR3 p.163–165)
  • Essence and Magic: every full point of Essence lost, or fraction of one, costs a point of Magic. Cyberware is the usual cause. (SR3 p.160)
  • Magic and the law: in the UCAS and CAS, any spell, spirit or focus of Force 3 or higher is legally regulated, and permits are available. Killing with magic is treated as premeditated. (MitS p.11)

Elven heritage

  • Tír Tairngire, “the Land of Promise” (SR3 p.30), takes up most of the former state of Oregon plus parts of Washington and California. (SR3 p.321)
  • It’s ruled by the Council of Princes, with High Prince Lugh Surehand. The Council was all-elven at first; it later admitted other metahumans, including the dragon Lofwyr. (SR3 p.30)
  • Rumors of immortal elves who lived through the last low-magic age are not covered in the core rulebook. Treat them as street talk.

Street survival

  • The megacorps hold extraterritoriality: their ground, their law. (SR3 p.22)
  • Without a SIN you have few civil rights, but you’re also harder to trace. See SINs & the SINless. (SR3 p.238)
