Contacts & Legwork

Asking a contact what they know, paying for it, searching the Matrix instead, and what each contact level adds. All rules are from the core rulebook, (SR3 p.61–62, p.253–256).

Legwork: asking a contact (SR3 p.253)

Legwork means following up clues by asking people. The book calls it “the primary function of most Shadowrun contacts”.

  1. Roll Etiquette. Make a Success Test with Etiquette (Street) or Etiquette (Corporate), whichever fits the contact, against TN 4. If the adventure has a success table for the question, use its target number. (SR3 p.253–254)
  2. Count successes. The number of successes decides what the contact knows and is willing to tell, which can be two very different things. More successes add to what fewer would give: 3 successes get the 1-, 2- and 3-success entries. (SR3 p.253)
  3. Play the meeting. The GM roleplays the encounter with that information level in mind. Contacts have their own lives and needs; a reporter may meet in a bar, a Mafia soldier in a back alley at 3 a.m. (SR3 p.253)
  4. Pay. See Paying for information.

Contacts stay trustworthy as long as the runners play it safe. Don’t reveal where the information came from, or make the contact choose between you and the people they work with. (SR3 p.253–254)

Writing a success table

Most published adventures include one. For your own mystery, write a line for each success count, from vague to specific. For example, asking a street shaman about a haunted block:

Successes The contact says
0 Nothing useful.
1 “The spirits around there are upset. Stay out after dark.”
2 “It’s a bound spirit, not a wild one. Somebody’s using it.”
3+ “A toxic shaman moved into the old water plant. That’s who’s binding it.”

The table above is an illustration, not a book table. In Foundry, the GM can keep one as a Rollable Table and let the 🔎 Legwork tool read it (see Legwork in The 2nd Chumming).

Paying for information (SR3 p.254)

Whether a contact charges, and when they’ll want a favor back, is the GM’s call. The book offers a formula:

Fee = contact’s Charisma × contact’s Intelligence × 50¥ ÷ successes on the Etiquette Test

The GM adjusts it to suit the contact, and normal Negotiation sets the final price (see Buying Gear & Fencing, step 4 of buying on the street, for how that test works).

Example. A street shaman contact with Charisma 4 and Intelligence 5. The runner rolls Etiquette (Street) against TN 4 and gets 2 successes: 4 × 5 × 50 = 1,000¥, ÷ 2 = 500¥ before negotiation. With 4 successes the same answer, and more, would cost 250¥.

“Keep an ear to the ground” (SR3 p.254)

A runner can ask a contact to check around and report back. The GM makes an Etiquette Test for the contact, at +2 to the success table’s target number. Any success means the contact reports that level of information in 2D6 hours, or whenever the GM decides. The fee uses the same formula.

This is the book’s way to make sure a clue the adventure needs reaches the runners.

Rushing a meeting (SR3 p.254)

Contacts aren’t always free when the runners want them. To rush a meeting, the runner makes an Etiquette Test to get it at all, and the Etiquette Test for the information then takes +2 to its target number.

Asking the right contact (SR3 p.254)

A contact only knows what someone in their line of work would know. A plainclothes cop hears precinct rumors but not a Mitsuhama secretary’s home address. A corporate contact may know patrol schedules and the latest secret project, but not a gang’s new turf.

The GM can make a contact “surprisingly multi-dimensional or disappointingly narrow”. A talismonger is a poor source for a gun; a chess partner might know a Knight Errant officer’s address. (SR3 p.256)

Searching the Matrix instead (SR3 p.254)

A decker can do legwork in the Matrix.

  • Test: Etiquette (Matrix) against the success table’s target number. Hacking Pool can’t be used.
  • Time: base time 2D6 hours. Successes can be traded to cut the base time instead of buying information.
  • What it finds: the decker says what kind of sources they search. Corporate databases give what a corporate contact would know; a street chat-line gives what a street contact would know.
  • How many at once: up to half Intelligence, rounded up, searches at a time, if the decker does nothing else. Other activity cuts that down at the GM’s discretion.

Contact levels (SR3 p.254–256)

Level Name What they’ll do Bonus on Etiquette Tests for information Covering for you when asked about the runner
1 Contact An acquaintance. “May know information that a character needs and will probably reveal it”, or may keep it to themself. — GM rolls Intelligence (6), opposed, for whether they can remember you. They won’t lie for you.
2 Buddy Sees you semi-regularly. Always provides the information, and may keep an ear to the ground if they have time. Loyal unless it means sticking their neck out too far. +1 die GM rolls Willpower (5), opposed, for whether they can refuse to answer.
3 Friend for life Knows you well and sees you regularly. +2 dice GM rolls Willpower (6), opposed, for whether they can refuse to answer.

A Level 1 contact who “can’t remember” the runner genuinely can’t, and if they do remember, they probably don’t know where to find you. (SR3 p.254–256)

Getting contacts (SR3 p.61–62, p.254)

  • Every character starts with two free Level 1 contacts. (SR3 p.61)
  • More can be bought only at character creation, on the Starting Character Extras Table. (SR3 p.62)
Extra contact Cost
Contact (Level 1) 5,000¥
Buddy (Level 2) 10,000¥
Friend For Life (Level 3) 200,000¥
  • After creation, contacts are gained only through roleplaying. New ones should be Level 1, rarely Level 2, and never Level 3. (SR3 p.254)

Contacts with lives of their own (SR3 p.256)

  • Flesh them out. Give each contact an archetype title (Street Doc, Detective) and something going on in their life: a business in trouble, a daughter in with the wrong crowd. It colors how they answer, and can start a run.
  • They can turn the tables: ask the runners for information, compete for the same data, be pressured into silence or into lying, or chat about weird happenings that turn out to be clues.
  • Favor for a friend. A contact may ask the runners to do a job as a favor rather than for nuyen, which lets the players pick their own runs.

Legwork in The 2nd Chumming

The GM runs legwork with 🔎 Legwork, one of the GM tools on the Rollable Tables sidebar tab. Players don’t have the button. Tell the GM who you’re asking and what about.

  1. Pick the runner and the contact. The contact list is that runner’s contacts. A Matrix search needs no contact.
  2. Pick what they do: ask the contact, have the contact keep an ear to the ground, or search the Matrix.
  3. Check the numbers. The dialog starts on Etiquette (Street), then Corporate, or Etiquette (Matrix) for a search. It adds the contact level’s bonus dice when asking. The TN starts at 4, or at the chosen success table’s. It adds +2 for a rushed meeting, +2 for an ear to the ground, and the runner’s wounds. The GM can change any number.
  4. Roll. On a TN of 7 or more, sixes are 💥 rerolls first. The result card shows the successes, every row of the success table up to that many, and the fee (Charisma × Intelligence × 50¥ ÷ successes) (SR3 p.253–254). An ear to the ground also shows the 2D6 hours; a Matrix search shows its base time and how many searches the decker can run at once. The card goes to the GM only unless the GM unticks that.

The contact’s Charisma and Intelligence come from its linked NPC or Little Black Book archetype. A contact with no stat block leaves them blank for the GM to fill in. The fee is only shown, never charged: Negotiation still sets the price.

Success tables. The GM: Legwork Success Tables compendium (GM Only folder) holds a template with rows for 1, 2 and 3+ successes. The GM imports a copy for each mystery, writes the rows, and puts the TN in its description as Target Number: N. Each row’s range is the success count that reveals it.

The rushed meeting’s own Etiquette Test isn’t rolled by the tool. Roll it from the sheet first.

Loyalty and Connection in The 2nd Chumming

The Foundry system’s contact item has Loyalty and Connection ratings (1–6). They aren’t in the SR3 core rulebook, which describes contacts only by level. The character importer sets both to the contact’s level, so Loyalty 1 / Connection 1 is a Level 1 contact. The 🔎 Legwork tool doesn’t store a level either: the GM picks Level 1, 2 or 3 in the dialog, and it starts at the contact’s Loyalty (3 at most). How the two ratings should map to the book’s levels is still open.

If you want the ratings to mean something, one light reading: use Connection to cap how far down the success table the contact can reach, and Loyalty to decide whether they’ll share anything that puts them at risk. That’s a suggestion, not a rule.
