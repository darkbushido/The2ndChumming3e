Buying Gear & Fencing

Availability, Street Index and Legality Codes; buying through contacts; and selling stolen goods to a fence. All rules are from the core rulebook, (SR3 p.237–238, p.270–274).

Original gist: “Gear Availability, Acquisition, & Fencing Guide”.

  • SR3 Legality Codes look like 6P-E, not “R” or “F”. The number is the restriction level, P means a permit is available, and the letter is the category.
  • Successes on the acquisition test divide the base time. They don’t cut it by 25–50% each.
  • Paying to hurry a search costs +2 days and +0.1 Street Index for each −1 to the Availability TN. It’s not “+25–50% per die”.
  • The price is negotiated: Negotiation against Intelligence, 5% per net success.
  • Fencing: the 30% base and 5% per net success were right, but the limits are a 10% floor and a 50% ceiling, not 60%. The “chop shop 10–15%” rate and the hot, encrypted and scarce percentage modifiers were invented. The book handles risk through the Finding a Fence TN instead.
  • The Ares Predator III isn’t in the core rulebook. The examples below use the core Ares Predator (450¥, Availability 3/24 hrs, Street Index .5).

Reading a gear entry (SR3 p.270–272)

Statistic Meaning
Concealability TN for Perception Tests to spot it. Searches use half.
Availability Two numbers: TN / base time, for example 3/24 hrs.
Cost The legal retail price in nuyen.
Street Index The black-market multiplier on Cost.
Legality Restriction level, permit availability, and category.

At character creation, no gear may have a Device Rating above 6 or an Availability above 8. (SR3 p.270) Gear modified for a dwarf costs +10%, and for a troll +25%. (SR3 p.272)

Buying on the street (SR3 p.272–273)

  1. Find a source. Contact a fixer or other suitable contact and make an Etiquette Test against the Availability TN. Contacts differ: a talismonger is great for magical goods and poor for guns.
  2. Wait. Base time ÷ successes is how long it takes the source to find the item.
  3. No successes? The contact can’t or won’t find it. At the GM’s discretion you can put the word out: for each −1 to the Availability TN, add 2 days to the wait and +0.1 to the Street Index. (SR3 p.273)
  4. Negotiate. Halfway through the wait you meet the source. The asking price is Cost × Street Index, plus any increase from step 3. You and the source each roll Negotiation against the other’s Intelligence. The winner moves the price 5% per net success in their favor. (SR3 p.273)
  5. Pick up. If you won’t pay, the deal is off, and the GM may raise the Availability TN next time you use that contact. (SR3 p.273)

The book’s example (SR3 p.273)

Cheshire wants a monofilament whip: Availability 24/14 days, Street Index 3, Cost 3,000¥. None of her contacts come close to TN

  1. She puts the word out, cutting the TN to 12. That adds 24 days (2 × 12) and +1.2 Street Index. She rolls 2 successes: (24 + 14) ÷ 2 = 19 days. The price is 3,000 × 4.2 = 12,600¥. She wins the negotiation by 4, which is 20% off, and pays 10,080¥.

Example: an Ares Predator

  • Ares Predator: 9M, SA, Availability 3/24 hrs, Cost 450¥, Street Index .5, Legality 6P-E. (SR3 p.278)
  • An Etiquette Test against TN 3 scores 2 successes, so the pistol turns up in 12 hours.
  • The asking price is 450 × .5 = 225¥ before negotiation. Its Street Index is below 1, so heavy pistols are cheaper on the street than in a store.

Legality Codes (SR3 p.273–274)

A code like 6P-E reads as follows:

  • 6 is the restriction level. The lower the number, the more tightly it’s controlled. When an officer notices the item, they roll their Security or Police Procedures skill against this number. With 1 success they know and may warn you. With more, they ask for a permit or arrest you. A low-enforcement area adds up to +3; a high-enforcement area subtracts up to −3, to a minimum of 2.
  • P means a permit is available. See SINs & the SINless.
  • E is the category on the Local Fines and Punishment Table.

Legal items have no code. They can be bought over the counter, but only in your own name, which leaves a data trail. (SR3 p.273)

Fencing the loot (SR3 p.237–238)

If the job came with an agreed way to dispose of the loot, these rules don’t apply. Otherwise, you need a fence.

1. Find a fence (SR3 p.237)

Make an Etiquette (Street) Test against TN 4, modified as below. A team rolls the average Etiquette (Street) of the group plus one die per member, and everyone who contributes must attend the meet.

Finding a Fence Table (SR3 p.238)

Situation Modifier
Using a regular contact −1
Disposing of standard gear −1
Disposing of hi-tech or other important loot +1
Disposing of hot loot +3
Being sought by the police +1
Being sought by a corp or organized crime +2
Magical loot (foci, spell formulae and so on) +2

Fences prefer, in this order: standard gear (weapons, armor, vehicles, jewelry, money), then high-tech loot (ordinary data, equipment, research), then hot loot (one-of-a-kind items, prototypes, secret files, magical items). (SR3 p.237)

2. Spend your successes (SR3 p.237–238)

You can split them between two uses:

  • Hurry it along: finding a fence takes a base 10 days, and each success allocated takes a day off, down to a minimum of 1 day. At the end of each day the GM rolls dice equal to the days spent so far against TN 6. Any success means the former owners have found out.
  • Finance the fence: the GM secretly rolls 2D6 × 100,000¥ and multiplies it by the successes allocated. That’s the most money the fence brings to the meet.

3. The meet (SR3 p.238)

  • One character and the fence each roll Negotiation against the other’s Intelligence, +2 because both sides are suspicious.
  • The base price is 30% of the item’s listed value. The winner shifts it 5% per extra success.
  • The fence can’t push it below 10%, and the team can’t push it above 50%.
  • The fence will bring muscle. If the former owners are hunting the loot, they may turn up too.

Example: five HK227 SMGs

  • The Heckler & Koch HK227 costs 1,500¥ each, so five are worth 7,500¥ at list. (SR3 p.278)
  • The fence’s base offer is 30%, which is 2,250¥.
  • The team’s face wins by 3 successes: +15%, for 45%, or 3,375¥.
  • Winning by 5 would reach 55%, but the ceiling holds it at 50%, which is 3,750¥.
