Legality Codes & Permits

What a Legality Code like 8P-V means, how to get restricted gear legally (a permit) and through a contact (the street), and what it costs if you’re caught with it. All rules are from the core rulebook, (SR3 p.270–274).

Reading a code (SR3 p.273)

A code has two parts: 8P-V is a restriction level (8), an optional P, and a category letter (V).

Part What it tells you
The number The severity of the restriction. “The lower the number, the higher the restriction level.” It is the target number an officer rolls against (below).
P Present: a permit is available, so you can own, carry or use it legally. Absent: no permit is issued.
The letter The category on the Local Fines and Punishment Table, which is what getting caught costs (below).

An item whose table prints “Legal” (or no Legality column at all) isn’t restricted: “For legal items, this isn’t a problem; characters can probably pick them up at the corner Stuffer Shack.” (SR3 p.273)

The code is Seattle / UCAS law. “An item’s legality may vary among federal UCAS agencies, other countries and the various extraterritorial megacorporations. What is illegal in Seattle may simply be controlled at Mitsuhama.” (SR3 p.273) The GM decides the code for anything the books leave out (military hardware, drones, vehicle-grade weapons).

When an officer notices (SR3 p.273)

In a casual encounter with law enforcement, or security staff empowered to act as law enforcement, the officer rolls their Security or Police Procedures Knowledge Skill against the restriction level as the target number. If the item is concealed, make a Perception Test first to see whether the officer notices it. In most cases the roll is only needed if the officer suspects or sees a restricted item.

Successes What happens
0 The officer notices nothing wrong.
1 The officer is aware, but won’t try to arrest you. They may issue a warning.
2 or more The officer presses the issue: asks for a permit, or tries to arrest you.

Enforcement varies. In a low-enforcement area the target number can go up by as much as +3 (“almost everyone looks the other way”). In a high-enforcement area it can drop by as much as −3, to a minimum of 2.

Getting it legally: the permit (SR3 p.273–274)

“The price listed for all items in SR3 are the legal retail prices. That means that you can get items for the price listed, but must prove you are legally allowed to own them.” A restricted item (one with a Legality Code) needs a permit to be bought legally, and only a code with a P can have one.

  1. Apply to the proper government or corporate authority and fill out the paperwork. The application needs a valid SIN, and may also need proof of employment and adequate reasons for the permit.
  2. Roll Etiquette against the item’s Availability + 2. Example from the book: an item with Availability 4 needs an Etiquette (6) Test.
  3. Time: the item’s base acquisition time divided by your successes.
  4. Price: 5% of the item’s price for a permit to possess; 10% for a permit to possess and transport.

Illustration (not from the book): a 1,000¥ item. A possess-only permit is 50¥, and possess and transport is 100¥, on top of the item’s own price.

A permit then makes the item easier to buy on the street: apply a −2 modifier to its Availability while you hold the appropriate permit. (SR3 p.274)

A permit is tied to an identity (SR3 p.273–274)

  • A permit is applied for with a SIN. Permits are not available to criminal SINs, or to the SINless.
  • A fake ID can be used to apply, but it must beat a Rating 6 verification system in an Opposed Test. (See SINs & the SINless for forging IDs.)
  • Legal purchases leave records: credit trails, inventory, perhaps video, depending on where you shop. Payment is expected in full at delivery, and keeping a legal identity has overhead of its own.
  • Makers and stores don’t want bad press. “If they discover that they’re being conned by shadowrunners, they will provide all pertinent records to the police,” which usually ends the forged legal documents’ usefulness for that character.

Getting it through a contact: the street (SR3 p.272–273)

The usual route for shadowrunners. “In order to purchase gear, characters must use an appropriate contact (see Contacts, p. 253).” Fixers are the usual middlemen, but other contacts suit other goods (a talismonger for magical items, say).

  1. Find a source. Roll Etiquette with a number of dice equal to the character’s Etiquette Skill, against the left number of the Availability Code (the target number). Success means a source is found and the order is placed. No successes means the contact can’t, or won’t, find it.
  2. Time. The right number is the base time (hours, days or months). It is divided by your successes. Halfway through, the face-to-face haggle takes place.
  3. Price. The street price is the item’s Cost × Street Index, plus anything added for hurrying the search. Source and buyer then make an opposed Negotiation vs Intelligence contest, and each net success moves the price 5%. If the buyer can’t or won’t pay, the deal is off.

Waiting longer (adding 2 days and +0.1 Street Index per −1 to the Availability target number) can buy back a failed search. Both of those, and the worked example with the monofilament whip, are on Buying Gear & Fencing.

The two routes compared:

  Legal purchase Through a contact
Price The listed (retail) price Cost × Street Index, haggled
Needs A valid SIN, and a permit if the item is restricted A contact, and a successful Etiquette roll
Leaves A paper and data trail None with the buyer, but the item is still restricted
Who can Someone with a clean SIN (and the permit) Anyone with a contact who can find it

What it costs to be caught (SR3 p.274)

Example: a Legality of 8P-V (Broadcast Encryption, Rating 1-4, SR3 p.290) is Class B Equipment: 4,000¥ for possession. The number 8 is the officer’s target number, and the P means a permit can be had.

In the Foundry system

The system follows the warn, never refuse rule. 🛒 Buy gear reads an item’s code and opens on “At the store” only for an item the book calls Legal. For anything restricted it opens on a contact, says so, and still lets the buyer pick either way (the GM may rule the character holds the permit). It doesn’t look permits up on a SIN or apply fines; the GM decides.
