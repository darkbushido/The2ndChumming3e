Instruction & Learning Skills

How a character learns a new skill or improves one: the Good Karma it costs, the training the gamemaster may require, a teacher with the Instruction skill, and the virtual instructor programs and chips sold on the Matrix. Karma costs are on the Karma page and aren’t repeated here. All rules are from the core rulebook, (SR3 p.87, p.95, p.245, p.296).

  1. Learning a new skill (SR3 p.245)
  2. The Instruction skill (SR3 p.87, p.95)
  3. Virtual instructors (SR3 p.95, p.296)
    1. Restricted skills (SR3 p.95)
    2. Legality (SR3 p.296)
  4. Skillsofts and chips (SR3 p.295–296)

Learning a new skill (SR3 p.245)

A new skill is bought at rating 1 for 1 Good Karma, whether it is an Active, Knowledge or Language skill. Raising it past 1 follows the improvement costs on Karma.

The gamemaster controls how fast. “Gamemasters should not allow characters to simply spend large amounts of Karma and become vastly more powerful almost overnight.” Characters should train, or use and improve their skills over time, and may need to search out a teacher (SR3 p.245). The book leaves the balance to the gamemaster and offers two ways:

Option How it works
A short, simple mechanic Make a Skill Test using the skill, against a target number of twice the Skill Rating sought. A character without the skill defaults. An instructor adds dice to this test (see below). Good Karma can be spent to learn or raise the skill only after this test succeeds.
Roleplay it Make the training the basis of an adventure: the instructor was just the target of a corporate extraction, a shaman’s totem demands a quest before the character can improve at conjuring.

Worked example (arithmetic from the rule, not from the book): a character wants Pistols 4. The test target number is 2 × 4 = 8. A teacher with the Instruction skill adds dice (below). Only after a success does the character spend the 6 Good Karma the Karma page works out for Pistols 4 at Quickness 4.

The Instruction skill (SR3 p.87, p.95)

Instruction is a Charisma-linked Active skill. It defaults to the Charisma Attribute, and its specializations are by specific subject (SR3 p.87).

  • To teach a skill, spell or other subject, the instructor must have that skill at Rating 3 or higher. They must also have it at or above the rating the pupil wants to reach (SR3 p.95).
  • Teaching requires an Instruction (4) Test. For every 2 successes, the student gets one additional die on the test to learn the skill (SR3 p.95).
  • The instructor’s effect is on the pupil’s learning test, so it pairs with the Skill Test option above.
  • Teaching spells works the same way. See Spells for the teacher’s fee and study time.

Virtual instructors (SR3 p.95, p.296)

“Numerous ‘virtual instructor’ programs are now available widely, especially through the Matrix.” Each is a program with a rating in the skill it teaches, and a rating in Instruction.

  1. Size. Add the two ratings together and read the Program Size Table (SR3 p.223) with a multiplier of 3. The size is the total squared, times 3.
  2. Price. For a program, use the Program Costs and Availability Table (SR3 p.304): the rating band sets the Availability, Street Index and cost per Mp of size.
  3. As a chip. A Virtual Instructor Chip is sold for Mp × 100¥, Availability (ratings total)/72 hrs, Street Index 1 (SR3 p.296).

Worked example (the sum and the squaring are from the book’s rule; the rest is the arithmetic): Pistols 4 and Instruction 3 give a total of 7. The Program Size Table’s rating 7 row is 49, and 49 × 3 = 147 Mp. As a chip: 147 Mp × 100¥ = 14,700¥, Availability 7/72 hrs, Street Index 1.

Restricted skills (SR3 p.95)

“Programs for illegal or restricted skills (such as Demolitions) typically have twice the Street Index and an Availability of 10 or higher.”

Legality (SR3 p.296)

The Virtual Instructor Chip’s Legality is printed as Legal/3-Y on the Skillsofts and Chips table. The category Y is on the fines table: see Legality Codes & Permits.

How the system reads the split. The book prints both values and doesn’t spell out when each applies. The system reads it as: Legal for an ordinary skill, 3-Y for the illegal or restricted skills that page 95 singles out. It’s recorded on the chips as “Legal (3-Y for illegal or restricted skills)”, and the GM decides which skills count.

Skillsofts and chips (SR3 p.295–296)

Virtual instructors are one of the products on the Skillsofts and Chips table. The others (Activesoft, Datasoft, Knowsoft, Linguasoft, Skillsoft Jukebox, Optical Memory Chip, Autonav Map Chip and the BTL chips) are sold as chips with their own price, Availability and Legality.

  • Softs don’t provide dice pools. A Pistols activesoft doesn’t give Combat Pool dice for Pistols Tests unless the character has the skill (SR3 p.296).
  • Magic skills such as Sorcery and Conjuring can’t be made into activesofts (SR3 p.296).
  • Memory a skillsoft takes up comes from the Program Size Table (SR3 p.223): a multiplier of 3 for a base skill and 2 for a specialization (SR3 p.296).
