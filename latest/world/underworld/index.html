The Underworld

The crime syndicates that run the shadow economy, how they fight each other, and what they want from runners, as of October 2059. Seattle is the example the books give in most detail.

New Seattle’s underworld chapter is written in character. The Mafia section is a stolen letter from a consigliere, and the Seoulpa section comes from leaked Lone Star files (NS p.97–106). Treat the details as what those sources believe.

Why the syndicates matter

The SR3 introduction says the Mafia, the Yakuza and the other syndicates “have grown explosively”, because their networks supply anything people will buy (SR3 p.8). In Seattle, the city’s position as a UCAS island inside NAN territory, near the sea route to Russia, makes it a major smuggling port for every syndicate (NS p.82). The syndicates hire runners through street Johnsons, who have less money than corp Johnsons but can offer protection, illegal services or extra bodies (MJLBB p.18).

The long war: Mafia against Yakuza (SR3 p.35; NS p.97–101)

Year What happened
2030 War breaks out in Seattle when the leaders of both syndicates are assassinated. After many deaths, the exhausted organisations call a truce (SR3 p.35). NS puts the ceasefire in 2031 (NS p.97).
2032 The yakuza in Japan send Korean bosses to rebuild the Seattle organisation (SR3 p.35).
2042–44 The Japanese yakuza bosses purge the Korean bosses they had sent to rebuild Seattle. The survivors form the Seoulpa Rings (SR3 p.35; NS p.101). The sources disagree on the year: SR3 says 2042, NS p.101 says 2043, and NS p.104 says 2044.
2044 Hanzo Shotozumi is made oyabun of Seattle. His strong-arm tactics bring the Mafia’s James O’Malley back to fight him (SR3 p.35).
2058 Don O’Malley is shot outside his home on New Year’s Day. An all-out mob war follows between the Mafia, Yakuza, Triads and Rings (SR3 p.35; NS p.97). NS says the killer worked for an organisation called Chimera (NS p.97).
2058–59 The yakuza seize large parts of Seattle’s criminal markets in the confusion (NS p.101). The Mafia families fight over who will lead Seattle, and the Commissione sends Don Leo “the Lion” McCaskill of Milwaukee to oversee the situation (NS p.97).

The Mafia (NS p.97–100)

It began on Seattle’s docks and in its labour unions, and was run from Chicago. Since O’Malley’s death, who will be capo of Seattle is the question of the moment. His daughter Rowena O’Malley took over the Finnigan Family within days and made a strong bid for it; Maurice “Butcher” Bigio is the other contender. The O’Malley camp believes Bigio arranged O’Malley’s death (NS p.97–98, p.100).

Family Leader Territory and business
Bigio Maurice “Butcher” Bigio Tacoma and the south: dock protection, smuggling, fencing, hijacking yakuza shipments, loan-sharking.
Finnigan Rowena O’Malley, a Harvard lawyer, with “Uncle Al” Cavalieri Downtown waterfront smuggling (shared with the yakuza and the Choson Ring), and Bellevue. Old Mary Finnigan opposes her.
Ciarniello Vince “Numbers” Ciarniello Everett and Snohomish: gambling at Casino Corner, smuggling on the Everett docks. He sides with whoever looks safest.

Conflicts: the families fight the yakuza first and each other second. An ambitious lieutenant might hire runners to do things the rest of the Family mustn’t know about (MJLBB p.53–54).

The Yakuza (NS p.100–102)

The yakuza followed the Japanese corps to Seattle. The Seattle clans answer to Hanzo Shotozumi, who was made oyabun of Seattle by Akira Watada of the powerful Watada-rengo in Japan and told to rebuild a traditional, loyal organisation. Since O’Malley’s death they have become the most powerful syndicate in the Pacific Northwest, and Shotozumi’s ambition has grown with them (NS p.101).

Group Leader Notes
Shotozumi’s organisation Hanzo Shotozumi Downtown and Tacoma: smuggling, gambling, computer crime. Its sokaiya branch works on corporations through stock purchases and blackmail.
Nishidon-gumi Isao Nishidon The oldest clan. Smuggling in Puyallup and Redmond, and the Barrens chip trade. Shotozumi’s main rival among the Seattle clans.
Shigeda-gumi Takeo Shigeda The smallest clan. Everett, Snohomish, and parts of Redmond. It is progressive, and admits more magicians and women.

Conflicts: Mitsuhama calls on the yakuza for its “special problems” (NS p.92–93). The sokaiya target corps in the Pacific Prosperity Group, which brings them into conflict with the Triads (NS p.102).

The Triads (NS p.102–104)

Chinese syndicates that control Hong Kong and most of mainland China. In Seattle they’re junior to the Mafia and the Yakuza, but rising: the city leans toward the PPG, which is opening trade with Hong Kong. Most of their money comes from smuggling. The Triads take metahumans and women more readily than other syndicates do (NS p.102; MJLBB p.54).

Triad Leader Notes
Yellow Lotus Lodgemaster Zheng Li Kwan, an initiated adept The largest. It recruits orks heavily and is strong in Puyallup. Runners fear its incense master, Su Cheng.
Eighty-Eights Rick Wu Modern, and heavily cybered. Gambling and vice in Everett, Bellevue and Puyallup. Allied with the Tigers gang.
Octagon David Gao The weakest. It has allied with the Yellow Lotus. Its incense master, Chen Kwan-Ti, is rumoured to work for the great dragon Lung.

The Seoulpa Rings (NS p.104–106)

Small Korean-led gangs descended from the survivors of the purge. Seattle has anywhere from a couple of dozen to fifty of them. They are the “wild cards”. Most hate the yakuza.

Ring Turf Business
Choson Downtown waterfront Dock smuggling. Fights the yakuza-backed First Nations gang.
Divine Revenge Bellevue Blackmail, influence peddling, fencing. It exists to destroy the yakuza.
Komun’go Redmond Smuggling, protection, information. Uses magic openly.
Phantom Lake Bellevue Smuggling across Lake Sammamish.
Red and Yellow Renton Deckers selling data, and protection rackets that use viruses.
Tartarus Puyallup Underground Smuggling and organlegging. Linked to the street drug shade.

Gangs

Below the syndicates are the street gangs, often allied with them. The Tigers work with the Eighty-Eights (NS p.103). The all-elf Ancients control smuggling from the Tir (NS p.106). New Seattle covers the major gangs from p.106 onward, and each district’s gangs in its district chapters.
