Megacorporations

The giants as of October 2059: who owns each one, where it’s based, what it does in Seattle, and what running against it is like.

New Seattle lists the megacorps and their Seattle offices (NS p.89–96), but it’s set a little later, in 2060. This page keeps the parts that hold for late 2059. Security descriptions are runners’ claims from the Shadowland posts in that book. They’re good GM material, but they aren’t statistics.

How they got here (SR3 p.31–35)

  • 2033: Damien Knight’s “Nanosecond Buyout”. A burst of computer-driven trades gave Knight 22% of Ares. He and CEO Leonard Aurelius loathed each other from the start (SR3 p.31).
  • 2034: Richard Villiers bought a failed tech company cheaply after its founders died, then used its “lost” research to buy his way into Fuchi (SR3 p.31).
  • 2037: Lofwyr revealed that he owned 63% of the company behind BMW, and renamed it Saeder-Krupp (SR3 p.31).
  • 2041–42: Yamatetsu forced its way onto the Corporate Court, turning the Big Seven into the Big Eight (SR3 p.31).
  • 2048: Operation Reciprocity. The corps struck Aztechnology jointly after Aztlan nationalised them (SR3 p.31).
  • 2058–59: The corp war begins. Miles Lanier left Fuchi for Renraku’s board, Fuchi’s three factions turned on each other, and Villiers formed Novatech to take his share out. Yamatetsu’s chairman died in 2059 and left his shares to his ork son; rather than depose him, Yamatetsu moved its headquarters to Vladivostok. Wuxing took Fuchi’s Corporate Court seat (SR3 p.34–35).

Ares Macrotechnology (NS p.89–90)

The military-industrial giant, led mostly by ex-military execs. In Seattle it avoids a costly war with Federated-Boeing and works with it instead, as a sub-contractor and ally. Its security subsidiary Knight Errant guards its buildings, and Ares Arms tests prototypes on KE. Its feud with Cross is personal: Leonard Aurelius, Knight’s old rival, sold his Ares stock and backed Cross, and his son now runs Cross’s Seattle division (NS p.91).

Aztechnology (NS p.81, p.90)

Banned from California and Tir Tairngire, so Seattle is where it pulls the strings on its business there, through shells and holding companies. At least one battalion of Aztlan troops is stationed in Seattle. Runners describe a layered magical defence of watcher spirits and then elementals, and rumours of blood magic in the sub-basements. Its Matrix host has a link to the Tenochtitlán home office. It is Aztlan’s corp in all but name, and relations between Aztlan and Seattle are “cool but cordial” (NS p.81).

Cross Applied Technologies (SR3 p.35; NS p.90–91)

A second-tier corp from Quebec pushing into the UCAS market, and paying for it: the Big Eight want no more newcomers, and Lucien Cross survived three assassination attempts in 2058 alone (SR3 p.35). Its black-ops division is the Seraphim. Runners say Cross prefers to take intruders alive, and its Matrix ice traces you so it can collect a favour later. Its people have been seen visiting the Draco Foundation.

The Draco Foundation

Set up by Dunkelzahn’s will to administer his bequests, with Nadja Daviar as chair (SR3 p.34). In Seattle it is run by Sassinian, a sasquatch. The Council of Princes refused it an office in the Tir, so the Seattle office covers the Tir as well. New Seattle calls it “a big-big-big employer of shadowrunners”: it has plenty of money and a will to carry out (NS p.91–92). It also sits on the UCC (NS p.83).

Fuchi Industrial Electronics (SR3 p.34–35)

A Big Eight computer giant tearing itself apart. Fuchi has three internal factions, and the corp war with Renraku has made their feud worse. In mid-2059 Fuchi accused Renraku of stealing its secrets through Miles Lanier, its former security chief; the charges were dropped. Richard Villiers, expecting the other factions to force him out, has formed Novatech and is quietly buying his Fuchi North America holdings into it. He has offered Lanier the job of Novatech’s head of security. Every faction, and every rival, wants to know what happens to Fuchi next, which means steady work for runners.

Mitsuhama Computer Technologies (NS p.92–93)

It aims for the top spot in computing while Fuchi and Renraku fight. Runners credit it with the “zero-zone”: zero penetration, zero survival. That means concealed autoguns, lethal gas, barghest guard dogs, bound elementals, and black ice that kills. Its wage mages are Unit 13, which is mostly human, with no shamans. Its Johnsons want the job done exactly as planned. For its “special problems” it can call on the Seattle yakuza.

Renraku Computer Systems (SR3 p.34–35)

Fuchi’s great rival, and lately the winner: months after Miles Lanier joined its board in 2058, Renraku began producing remarkable technological advances. Fuchi’s espionage charges in mid-2059 were dropped, but Lanier left Renraku and sold his stock to the Zurich-Orbital bank. In Seattle, Renraku is the Arcology: a self-contained corporate city that houses nearly 100,000 people (NS p.30).

Saeder-Krupp (SR3 p.31; NS p.94–95)

Owned by the great dragon Lofwyr, and called the world’s largest megacorp. Officially it has no presence in Seattle. Unofficially it has subsidiaries, small offices, and watchers posted by Lofwyr. “The biggest business Saeder-Krupp does in Seattle is shadow business,” and nobody can predict what it will want.

Shiawase (NS p.76, p.95–96)

The Shiawase decision that created extraterritoriality is named after this corp (SR3 p.22). Shiawase Atomics maintains Seattle’s power grid, and wants Gaeatronics’s generating contract (NS p.76). Its spy network often knows a run is coming. Its defences use net-guns and knockout gas, and its ice is mostly trace, because it wants prisoners to interrogate.

Wuxing (SR3 p.35; NS p.81, p.96)

A small family corp until Dunkelzahn’s money raised it to the top. In 2059 Wuxing took the Corporate Court seat left empty when Fuchi’s representative died (SR3 p.35). It drives the Pacific Prosperity Group, a trade bloc against Japanese economic dominance. That makes it an enemy of the Japanese corps, and a rival of Federated-Boeing, which is also in the PPG. Runners expect the Triads to work on its behalf.

Yamatetsu (SR3 p.35; NS p.96)

When chairman Tadamako Shibanokuji died in 2059, his shares went to his son Yuri, an ork. Pressed to depose him, Yamatetsu instead moved its headquarters to Vladivostok (SR3 p.35). It now sells itself as the meta-friendly, “Awakened” corp, and its traditionalist staff are fighting that change. It has no friends among the other Japanese corps (NS p.96).
